Home Sample Collection in Delhi NCR

A clear, human-assisted booking process—from the first call to report delivery.

Home collection is available from 6:00 AM to midnight on all days. Same-day and advance booking are possible, while advance booking is preferred for early-morning slots.

The usual service footprint extends up to approximately 35 km from the GK-I laboratory. Serviceability, current charges and the available time are confirmed before the booking is accepted.

The current policy does not levy a collection charge when the bill value exceeds ₹1,000. This is a price-policy item and should be reconfirmed before launch.

Convenient scheduling

Thirty-minute appointment slots, subject to location and availability.

Identified phlebotomist

Uniform, ID card and visit details shared before arrival.

Barcode at the residence

Samples are labelled for the patient at the point of collection.

Insulated transport

Samples are transported in insulated boxes with ice packs.

How the service works

01

Call or WhatsApp

Share patient details, address, requested tests and preferred date and time.

02

Confirm preparation

Fasting, test timing and other test-specific instructions are checked before collection.

03

Collect and label

The phlebotomist verifies details, collects the sample and applies the patient barcode.

04

Transport and accession

The sample travels to the laboratory and is checked for identity and suitability.

05

Test, review and deliver

Testing follows departmental workflow; reports are delivered through the selected digital or print channel.

Frequently asked

Can I book for the same day?

Yes, subject to route and phlebotomist availability.

Do I need to fast?

Only when the requested test or clinical instruction requires it. Confirm before the visit.

Can I cancel?

No advance is normally collected. Cancellation is possible before test processing begins; final policy wording requires approval.

Can you guarantee an exact arrival time?

No. A slot is confirmed, but traffic and operational factors can affect arrival. The patient should be informed if a delay occurs.
